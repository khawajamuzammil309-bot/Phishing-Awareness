# CodeAlpha_Phishing-Awarness
The presentation (15 slides) created is called ‘Phishing Awareness Training: Don’t Take The Bait’. This presentation has been created to educate the audience about phishing and how to avoid it. It uses a dark navy, bright teal and red design to keep the audience engaged throughout. The presentation starts by providing a title slide which shows the objective of the presentation which is to create a human firewall. The presentation then defines what phishing is and how it works using a 3 step process to show the hook, trap and catch. The presentation then moves onto the different types of phishing there are to be aware of, this includes Email Phishing, Spear Phishing, Whaling, Smishing, Vishing and Clone Phishing. The presentation then shows 7 red flags to watch out for when communicating with someone. The presentation includes a detailed breakdown of what a phishing email looks like, including highlighting the spoofed email address, mismatched links and urgency.



The presentation then shows how to spot phishing emails. It compares genuine websites with fake ones and teaches users how to identify what they look like, how to spot typosquatting and how to see if the padlock is secure. The presentation goes on to explain why people fall victim to social engineering. Fear, urgency, curiosity, authority and greed. The presentation includes 3 examples of real world scenarios, this includes the Google Docs OAuth 2017, PayPal invoices and Office 365. The presentation then goes onto show what can happen if you fall victim to a breach. This includes losing money, stolen credentials and ransomware being put in place. The presentation concludes by listing 10 golden rules to follow when trying to avoid phishing. This has been split into 2 slides. Multi factor authentication, out of band verification and checking links. The presentation also concludes by showing a 4 step process to follow if you have accidentally clicked on a link. The presentation ends with a quiz containing 5 scenarios. This has been split into 3 slides.
